## 2. Verified facts (recording / processing)
- **Omni room is "the sound of the record."** Every *Absolutely* vocal except "The Dress" was an AKG C414 set to **omni**, through a Heritage 1073-style preamp, gain "as hot as possible without it initially clipping" (Reverb). Most of it was recorded with a single central mic in the room (Gallery&Studio).
- **Thin + boxy, mids cut.** "The reason a lot of the music sounds so thin is because a lot of it was done actually picking up room, in a very boxy room… [they] take out most of the mids with a graphic EQ" (Reverb).
- **Record it immediately, don't optimise it.** Ableton, Octatrack breakbeats, a little Eurorack (Strega on "Talk Down", Rings loop on "Did You See It?"). The rule was "not trying to make them sound as good as possible before you record" (Reverb).
- **Minimal mix.** Jack Karaszewski mixed *Absolutely* in Ableton with "a few key plugins": riding volumes, "learn[ing] how to use compressors… for the first time", "varying fidelities" (Reverb).
- **Gestural noise.** Feedback swells open "Big Mike's". "Gestural explosions" of feedback and slide stabs. On tour, noise went through a VCA that opened only when he touched a sensor strip, giving "harsh noise sweeps" between phrases (Reverb).
- **Playing style.** "You won't really catch us strumming much. It's a lot of jumpy picking" (Reverb).
- **Bleed is the vocal "processing" on *Baby*.** He used a 414 in the garage or an SM57 with "the mains as loud as they can go… you're hearing bleed… the entire song coming through", plus stacked freestyle vocals. Jams used an Octatrack, an MPC and a TR-8, and Dijon finished the record alone in his garage over about two months (Tommy King via MusicRadar).
- **Baby credits.** Dijon plays fuzz guitar on "Yamaha", Henry Kwapis plays fuzz guitar on "Another Baby!", and Dijon plays resonator guitar on "Loyal & Marie". Tommy King plays harmonizer on "Fire!". Sarlo mixed most tracks, Mk.gee mixed 2–3 and BJ Burton mixed 11 (Wikipedia/Tidal).
- **Arranging by muting.** "You take all these sources and try one pass of just muting s***… then we don't touch it anymore" (Vice). The album was assembled from abandoned scraps: "my thing is mistakes" (P&P).
- **Critics' descriptions of *Baby*.** "Reverb is applied so generously that you are aware of every sound's echo". The acoustic guitar is "chopped up and blown out". There are "overdriven frequencies" on "FIRE!" and "endless delay applied to every drum hit in '(Freak It)'". "Kindalove" is "gradually drowned in reverb". Prince's "Dorothy Parker", recorded on a console with no top end, is named as a touchstone (Pitchfork). Instruments are "reversed and gated", there are "FM-radio rips" and re-amped, "fried" piano, and "my man" sounds "played through a defective tape deck, the cassette skipping and the speakers blown out" (New Yorker). "Another Baby!" has "blown-out guitars" on a stuttering vocal sample (FADER).
- **Co-producer aesthetic.** Mk.gee: "I like underwater-sounding things… I grew up on 12-bit music"; "cheap, bad gear" (MusicRadar).

## 3. Sonic signature in measurable terms (all numbers **[inference]** from the facts above plus listening conventions)
- **EQ:** a mid scoop (about −6 to −10 dB, 300 Hz–1.5 kHz) and a thin, low-cut room (HPF about 100–150 Hz). On *Baby*, the top is often rolled off (LPF about 4–6 kHz) on "tape/Dorothy Parker" moments.
- **Saturation:** a preamp driven hot, so mostly 2nd/3rd-harmonic crunch. On *Baby* the bus and instruments are clipped into "blown-out" fuzz (+30–40 dB of fuzz gain on guitar). A compressor slammed into distortion makes it sound like "inside the speaker".
- **Compression:** inexperienced, heavy, pumping. Fast attack, 8:1 or more on room/bleed, so the room swells between notes.
- **Room/reverb:** on *Absolutely*, a short boxy room (about 0.3–0.6 s) that is always present, because the mic is omni and everything bleeds. On *Baby*, long reverbs of about 3–8 s, sometimes self-reflecting walls of reverb ("Kindalove").
- **Delay:** dub-style repeats of about 150–250 ms with high feedback (about 0.8) on hits ("(Freak It)").
- **Pitch/modulation:** no clean chorus. Wobble comes from tape and cheap gear (about ±5–15 cents, 0.5–1 Hz). Stacked takes give natural 10–30 cent spreads; in the MusicRadar quote this "million different vocals" stacking plays the role of a doubler. The harmonizer on "Fire!" means pitch-shifted layers.
- **Gating/chopping:** reversed-and-gated stabs, sudden cuts to silence, stutter edits on the beat or at irregular, tempo-free rates.
- **Lo-fi:** FM/AM-radio band-limiting, cassette skips and dropouts, 12-bit "underwater" artefacts.
- **Stereo:** mostly a mono room image (one central omni mic) with wide stacked layers on top.
- **Dynamics:** sparse, "jumpy" single-note picking and swells, not strums.

## 4. Reference songs
- **"Big Mike's"** (*Absolutely*): feedback swells, Noah Le Gros slide, an omni-room band take, a thin boxy mix (Reverb; Gallery&Studio).
- **"Many Times" / "Rodeo Clown"** (*Absolutely*): spare, jumpy picking. The room is the reverb and the raw vocal is driven hot through the 73 (Reverb; Pitchfork 2022).
- **"Another Baby!"** (*Baby*): Kwapis on fuzz guitar, blown-out guitars over a stuttering sample, vocals clipped into silence (Wikipedia; FADER; New Yorker).
- **"Baby!"** (*Baby*): acoustic guitar "chopped up and blown out" (Pitchfork).
- **"FIRE!"** (*Baby*): overdriven frequencies, a harmonizer and IDM drums (Pitchfork; Wikipedia; New Yorker).
- **"my man" / "Kindalove"** (*Baby*): defective-tape-deck R&B on the first, and a sunny song drowning in reverb on the second (New Yorker; Pitchfork).

## 5. WARMFUL recipes
```
name: "DIJ Omni Room"
description: "Absolutely spare room: thin, mid-scooped, hot preamp, always-on boxy room. Pick sparse single notes."
effects: [SWEETEN, DOUBLER, SPACE, FILTER]
moduleOn: [true, true, true, true]
order: [DIFF, CHAR, MOVE, TEXT]
primary:   [0.58, 0.55, 0.15, 0.22, 0.06, 0.38, 1.0, 0.60]
secondary: [0.65, 0.50, 0.20, 0.50, 0.05, 0.50, 0.50, 0.50]
filterStyle: Tilt
why:
- DIFF SPACE chamber (~0.45 s, 38 % wet) FIRST: the omni C414 captured the boxy room along with the source; the room is part of the signal hitting the preamp (Reverb).
- CHAR SWEETEN, SENS 0.65, TILT 0.58: Heritage 73 "as hot as possible without clipping", with the -1.5 dB @450 Hz dip and a brighter tilt for the "thin" sound (Reverb).
- MOVE DOUBLER ~17 ms, low level, drift ~20 c: bleed of other players/takes in the one-mic room, not a chorus (Reverb, MusicRadar/King).
- TEX FILTER Tilt 0.60 (gentle high-pass): stands in for the graphic EQ that "took out most of the mids" and thins the low end (Reverb). WARMFUL has no mid-scoop, so HP + bright tilt approximate it [inference].
```

moduleOn: [true, true, true, true]

secondary: [0.55, 0.40, 0.35, 0.50, 0.30, 0.50, 0.35, 0.50]
filterStyle: Tilt
why:
- CHAR FUZZ, round (TILT 0.42): fuzz guitar credited on "Yamaha" and "Another Baby!", "blown-out guitars" (Wikipedia; FADER).
- MOVE DOUBLER ~22 ms, drift ~28 c: stacked "million different vocals"/layers instead of clean width (MusicRadar/King).
- DIFF SPACE ~4–5 s, drift 0.3: "reverb applied so generously… slow-motion decay" (Pitchfork).
- TEX SQUASH 0.76 (into tanh overdrive): the reverb tail gets crushed and pumps, "listening from inside the speaker" (Pitchfork). OUTPUT 0.35 compensates.

why:

- MOVE TREMOLO square (AMT 0.85) ~4.6 Hz, drift 0.35: hard gate/stutter with irregular timing, "seconds added and subtracted" (Pitchfork); the VCA "noise sweeps" gesture (Reverb).
- TEX INTERFERENCE 0.28: AM/FM band-limiting and whistle, the "FM-radio rips" (New Yorker).

## 6. Confidence and open questions
- **High confidence:**
  - the omni C414 room recording, the hot 73-style preamp, the mid scoop and the minimal mix on *Absolutely* (primary Dijon quotes);
  - bleed-as-processing on *Baby* (Tommy King quote);
  - the credited fuzz guitar, resonator guitar and harmonizer.
- **Medium confidence:** *Baby*'s reverb, gating, reversal, tape-deck and radio effects. These are critics' descriptions, not engineer statements. No interview with Sarlo, BJ Burton, Kwapis or Karaszewski names specific plugins, consoles, tape machines or reverbs; the Sarlo Tape Op #134 interview is paywalled and predates *Baby*.
- **Inference:** all Hz, ms, cents and dB values, and the mapping of "graphic-EQ mid cut" to a high-pass plus bright tilt. WARMFUL has no parametric mid cut.
- **Open:**
  - which fuzz pedals Dijon and Kwapis used (overlaps with the DijonRig brief);
  - whether "chopped" acoustic guitar means DAW edits or a hardware gate;
  - whether "FM-radio rips" are literal radio samples or processing;
  - a DIJ "Kindalove" cloud-reverb preset (SPACE TIME 0.85–1.0) could replace "Freak It Echo" if the pack needs fewer delay presets. It might overlap with MKG Bowed Pad.
- **Duplication check:** "DIJ Omni Room" deliberately differs from MKG Distorted Room. It uses SWEETEN plus a high-pass, not DRIVE plus SQUASH, and its room is short.
